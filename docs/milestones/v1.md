## Measurements

- Scan to first translation on screen: **40 ms**; all 12 nodes of a screen
  translated and rendered within **170 ms**.
- Six rapid swipes produced 5 scans and 0 redundant provider calls; overlays for
  text that scrolled away were removed (3 Hide events, no ClearAll).

## Platform constraints found on device

These are Android behaviours, not defects. They bound what V1 can do:

- **Full opacity and touch passthrough are mutually exclusive.** Android caps a
  touch-passthrough overlay at `maximum_obscuring_opacity_for_touch` (0.8 by
  default) because a see-through-but-unclickable overlay is the shape of a
  tapjacking attack. Removing `FLAG_NOT_TOUCHABLE` restores full opacity but
  makes the whole screen unclickable and unscrollable. Verified by experiment in
  both configurations. Passthrough was chosen, so roughly a fifth of the source
  text shows through. A window-level alpha cannot be compensated for in the view
  hierarchy — it applies after the window is composited.
- **Matching the source background is impossible in V1.** The leftover strip
  where a translation is shorter than its source shows `0.8 × ours + 0.2 ×
  theirs`, so a background close to the app's own would blend in — but
  `AccessibilityNodeInfo` exposes no colours and sampling the screen needs
  MediaProjection, which V1 excludes.
- **Settings-style apps hide overlays entirely** via
  `setHideOverlayWindows(true)`. Acquisition and translation still run; only
  rendering is suppressed.
- **Passwords never reach the pipeline as plaintext.** Verified on device: a
  17-character password is exposed to accessibility as 17 `U+2022` bullets. The
  masked-text rule in `DefaultSensitiveContentPolicy` covers that form, and the
  password setup screen exposes nothing to accessibility at all.

## Third-party verification

Verified against Chromium rendering `docs/testing/reading-sample.html` — a
text-heavy English page. WebView matters because its accessibility tree comes
from Chromium, not from code in this repository.

It immediately exposed two real defects that native UI had hidden (both fixed):
type size was derived from bounds height as though that were a line height, so a
four-line paragraph collapsed onto one illegible line, and a container node
reporting the height of the whole WebView produced display-sized type across
half the screen.

After the fix: 7 nodes for the first screen, paragraphs rendered multi-line at
readable size, and 4 swipes produced 6 scans, 5 Hide events and no ClearAll.
A sentence repeated twice on the page kept two independent overlays, so identity
by occurrence index holds under Chromium's tree as well.

## Outstanding

- **Touch passthrough** is not separately verified on device. It rests on
  `FLAG_NOT_TOUCHABLE`, whose semantics the platform guarantees, and the opacity
  cap above is indirect evidence that the system treats the window as
  touch-passthrough.

## Operational notes

- Reinstalling the APK leaves the service listed as enabled and even "bound",
  but it stops receiving events. It must be toggled off and on again.
- `am force-stop` kills the service and the system does not restart it.
- Enabling the service through `settings put` is unreliable; granting it through
  the system UI works.
